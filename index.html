    How this works

    Why there are no commercial games here

    Cartridge games from the eighties and nineties are still under copyright, and copying them is not made legal by the passage of time. This site therefore hosts only work whose authors have released it under a licence that permits sharing, and every entry in the library names its licence and links back to the original release.

    For anything else, use the loader above: your own files stay on your own machine, which is the one arrangement that is nobody's business but yours.

    Controls

    Keyboard by default:

    ↑↓←→ d-pad · X A · Z B · Enter start · Shift select

    Plug in a USB or Bluetooth gamepad and it is picked up automatically. On a phone or tablet, an on-screen pad appears.

    Saving

    The player supports save states: freeze exactly where you are, come back later. States are kept in your browser's own storage, so they stay on the device you made them on. You can also export a state to a file and load it anywhere.

    Privacy

    There are no accounts, no analytics and no trackers. A game file you load is handed straight to the emulator running in your tab. It is not uploaded, not logged and not retained.

    Performance

    The 8-bit and 16-bit consoles run comfortably on almost anything, phones included. Nintendo 64 and PlayStation ask considerably more of the machine and are best on a desktop with hardware acceleration switched on.
